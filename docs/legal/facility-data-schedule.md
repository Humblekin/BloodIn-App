## 1. Permitted purpose

Access to BloodIn organization tools is limited to the organization's
authorized health-related and donation-coordination purposes, such as
managing staff memberships and permissions and recording or viewing
authorized donation-program information. The parties must list all specific
purposes and exclude unrelated marketing, profiling, employment decisions,
or onward disclosure unless separately lawful and documented.

## 2. Information and affected people

Depending on the organization's features, information can include account
identifiers, names, email addresses, staff roles, blood group, donor
availability, request and response information, donation records, donor-card
details, location, and communications. It may concern donors, requesters,
staff, and other users.

Before activation, the parties must document the exact data fields, purposes,
access groups, legal grounds, notices, and any sensitive-data consent or other
required authorization.

## 3. Organization responsibilities

The organization will:

- appoint only staff who need access and promptly remove access when it is
  no longer needed;
- give staff documented instructions and privacy/safety training;
- use information only for the agreed purpose and applicable law;
- keep information accurate and correct errors through authorized workflows;
- protect credentials and report suspected compromise promptly;
- not export, copy, disclose, sell, or use information for unrelated
  purposes;
- respond to data-subject requests in coordination with BloodIn; and
- maintain any required notices, lawful grounds, consents, clinical records,
  and independent record-retention duties.

## 4. BloodIn operator responsibilities

The operator will document the services and access controls provided, use
organization information only as authorized and for service operation, keep
an up-to-date vendor/subprocessor list, and provide a contact for privacy and
security matters. The final agreement must state how the operator supports
access, correction, deletion, incident response, audits, retention, and
return or deletion of organization information.

Do not treat these statements as a representation that all controls are
already implemented. Confirm actual production configuration and operational
procedures before execution.

## 5. Security incidents and requests

Each party will notify the other promptly at **[incident contact]** after
becoming aware of suspected unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, or
alteration affecting information covered by this schedule. The parties will
preserve relevant evidence, cooperate on containment and assessment, and
coordinate any legally required notifications. The final agreement must set
operational timelines and decision-making responsibilities consistent with
applicable law.

The organization will promptly forward requests from individuals about
organization-held information to **0599963155** and will not promise a
response outcome before coordinating with the other party.

## 6. Access end, retention, and deletion

On termination, the organization will stop using BloodIn and identify any
records it must keep under law. The parties will document which information
BloodIn deletes, returns, retains, or de-identifies, and the timelines for
production systems, backups, logs, shared records, and statutory records.
Those periods have not yet been set and must be completed before this
schedule is used.

## 7. Vendors, transfers, and further processing

The operator must disclose the production Supabase region and relevant
subprocessors, Paystack payment flows where applicable, authentication email
delivery, and any other service providers before an organization uploads
real data. The parties must assess any cross-border processing and comply
with applicable requirements.

## 8. Schedule completion

The parties must attach a data inventory, authorized role list, retention
table, vendor list, security contact tree, and applicable privacy notice
before signing.
